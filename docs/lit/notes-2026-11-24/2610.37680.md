# 2610.37680: Artifact Distillation: Training-Free Transfer from Frontier to Small Language Models

arXiv 2610.37680v1, 12 Oct 2026, cs.CL. 10 pages (4 main, 6 appendix and references). Mustafa Safa Ozdayi, Somin Wadhwa. Core AI, Qualtrics. No code link in the paper.

In one line: a strong teacher solves training problems and writes short text "artifacts" (trigger plus advice); a frozen student picks artifacts by trigger and reads them before answering. Text is the only form tested. The artifacts come from the teacher's own solves, not from the student's failures, and are never checked against a student re-run.

## Claim (key numbers and where they are)

1. With an Opus 4.5 raw bank, Haiku 4.5 beats unaided Sonnet 4.5 on spatial, 0.707 vs 0.647, at 60% of Sonnet's cost ($6.7 vs $11.2 per 1,000 instances). On tablejoin it reaches 0.675 vs Sonnet's 0.698 at 89% of the cost (Abstract, §4, Table 1, shaded cells).
2. The same raw bank lifts Qwen3.5-27B pooled from 0.730 to 0.800, t = 2.3 (Table 1). Transfer is not limited to the Claude family.
3. Pooled raw-bank gains shrink for stronger Claude students: Haiku +0.071 (t = 3.9), Sonnet +0.020 (t = 1.1, not significant), Opus +0.026 (t = 2.0, borderline) (§4, Table 1).
4. The teacher to student gap does not predict transfer. Haiku's gap is about the same on connections (0.240) and spatial (0.233), but the bank recovers 52% of the spatial gap and 0% of connections. math_comp has the largest gap (0.386) and recovers 0% (§4.1, Table 2).
5. An artifact helps only when it recurs across instances AND supplies a step the student skips (§4.1).
6. Cleaning the bank with an LLM judge hurts Haiku: pooled gain falls from +0.071 to +0.039, driven by tablejoin (-0.060) and spatial (-0.067) (§4.2).
7. A bank costs $0.57 to $1.18 to build. On spatial, Haiku plus bank pays back the $0.75 bank cost after about 170 queries vs unaided Sonnet (App. B, Table 3).

I re-derived the arithmetic: 0.120/0.233 = 52%, 0.177/0.299 = 59%, 6.7/11.2 = 60%, 8.9/10.0 = 89%, 0.75/(0.0112 - 0.0067) = 167 queries, 243 - 169 = 74 rejected. All match.

## Method, in enough detail to reproduce

Objects (§2). Teacher T = Opus 4.5. Student S. Task with D_train and held-out D_eval. Transfer = student accuracy on D_eval with bank minus the same student without bank.

Teacher pass (§2, App. A.1, A.2).
1. For each training instance, T solves it and writes one or more artifacts in one call. Each artifact = id, one-line trigger, free-text content. No fixed format: prose, checklist, worked pattern.
2. The prompt says the student will see different instances, so artifacts must be domain-general, must not name entities, values or fixed solution sequences, and should be few and broad.
3. If T's answer is not fully correct, a reflection call shows T the ground truth and asks for new artifacts. These replace the first-attempt artifacts.
4. The teacher never sees a student. One bank serves all students.

Bank cleaning (§2, App. A.3). Opus 4.5 as judge reads the whole bank as JSONL in one call and returns KEEP or REJECT per artifact with a reason: LEAKAGE, DUPLICATE (cite the kept id), TRIVIAL, NOT_ACTIONABLE. It may not rewrite artifacts.

Student pass (§2, App. A.5 to A.7).
1. Selection call: the student sees the problem and a manifest of id | trigger only, content withheld. It returns the smallest set of ids that would change what the solver does, or NONE. Told not to solve.
2. Answer call: a fresh call gets the problem plus the full content of the selected artifacts, with the system line "strategies distilled from completing similar tasks. Use them where they apply."
3. Baseline: same answer prompt with no artifacts (A.4).

Settings. All models non-thinking, temperature 1 (§3 footnote 1). Five runs per eval instance per configuration, averaged.

## Setting, data, models

Tasks (§3, App. B). LiveBench, instances not retired as of August 2026. Six tasks screened by unaided Opus minus Haiku gap; the largest significant gap kept per category, which dropped AMPS_Hard and cta. Kept: connections (group 12 to 16 words into fours, gap 0.240), math_comp (competition math, gap 0.386), spatial (count pieces after cutting solids or polygons, gap 0.233), tablejoin (map columns when one table's names are hashed, F1, gap 0.299).

Splits (Table 3). About 40/60 train/eval, stratified by subtype; math_comp also by topic; near-duplicates kept on one side. connections 20/30, math_comp 17/29, spatial 20/30, tablejoin 20/30. Total eval = 119 instances.

Teacher train accuracy: .79, 1.00, .85, .68. Bank size raw to clean: 69 to 52, 38 to 29, 59 to 38, 77 to 50.

Models. Teacher and judge: Opus 4.5. Students: Haiku 4.5, Sonnet 4.5, Qwen3.5-27B (local, no cost), and Opus 4.5 on its own bank.

## Metrics (exact definitions)

- Accuracy = each task's official LiveBench grader score (tablejoin is F1), averaged over 5 runs per instance (§3).
- Cost = dollars per 1,000 instances at Anthropic list prices (August 2026), summed over the selection and answer calls. Bank-building cost is not in this number (§3, Table 1).
- Pooled = all 119 eval instances. t = paired t statistic (mean over standard error) of the per-instance accuracy difference vs the same model with no bank. |t| > 2 is called 95% significant (Table 1 caption).
- Gap = Opus minus Haiku unaided accuracy. Rec. = share of that gap recovered by the raw bank for Haiku (Table 2).
- Sel. = share of eval runs where Haiku selected the artifact (Table 2, App. C).

Positive.
- Haiku spatial +0.120, tablejoin +0.177 with raw bank.
- Qwen tablejoin +0.263 (0.408 to 0.671), its weakest task.
- Opus spatial +0.080 on its own bank.
- Mechanism evidence (§4.1): on spatial, share of Haiku answers that trace each region's boundary goes 23% to 46%. On tablejoin Haiku returns 3.2 mappings per problem instead of 2.2 (gold has 3.2); recall .47 to .70, precision .61 to .68.

Weak and negative (several are in the table but not discussed in the text).
- connections: no gain for Haiku (-0.010 raw, -0.022 clean). 87% of unaided answers already test the top artifact's idea. Secondary-sense checks go 6% to 21% with no accuracy gain.
- math_comp: no gain. The selector returns NONE on 75% of runs; top artifact selected 10%.
- Sonnet math_comp drops 0.586 to 0.538 with the raw bank (-0.048). This is the largest single drop in the table and the paper does not mention it.
- Opus connections drops 0.916 to 0.897 with the raw bank.
- Sonnet gains are not significant (t = 1.1 raw). Opus clean bank t = 0.4.
- Sonnet's cost with the bank nearly doubles ($13.6 to $22.9) for +0.019 pooled. The bank roughly doubles Haiku cost too ($4.4 to $7.7).
- Per-task significance is never reported. Only pooled t.

Cleaning (§4.2).
- Judge rejects 74 of 243 (30%); 58 as duplicates.
- On tablejoin, three of Haiku's four most-selected artifacts are rejected as duplicates of the fourth. With raw bank, the "match columns by values" advice appears 3.9 times per instance; with clean bank 0.9 times. Haiku then selects contradictory artifacts together on 92% of clean-bank runs vs 31% raw. Precision falls .68 to .62.
- On spatial, the judge keeps the terser artifact in 11 of 16 duplicate pairs. 38% of rejected duplicates contain a concrete example vs 16% of kept ones. Authors call the spatial losses individually non-significant.
- Qwen loses on no task with the clean bank and gains +0.018 pooled. So the effect of cleaning depends on the model and task.

## What each figure and table shows

- Figure 1 (p. 2): a schematic, no data. Left: teacher solves a training task, writes artifacts into a bank, reflects on ground truth if wrong; grayed artifacts are removed for leakage or duplication. Right: frozen student selects ids from triggers only, then a second call gets the selected artifacts in full. Matches the text.
- Figure 2 (p. 2): one tablejoin artifact (a1) as a card: trigger visible during selection, content revealed after. Illustrative only.
- Table 1 (p. 3): accuracy and cost per task and pooled, 4 students by 3 bank conditions. Means of 5 runs; no per-task error bars or CIs. Pooled t only. Shaded cells are Haiku raw vs unaided Sonnet on spatial and tablejoin. The table supports the headline. It also holds the unmentioned drops above.
- Table 2 (p. 4): one of Haiku's most-selected artifacts per task with its source training problem, Sel., Gap, Rec. spatial 53% / .233 / 52%; tablejoin 97% / .299 / 59%; connections 68% / .240 / 0%; math_comp 10% / .386 / 0%. Shows that selection rate alone does not predict recovery (connections is selected 68% and recovers nothing).
- Table 3 (p. 9): split sizes, teacher train accuracy, bank size raw to clean, bank cost. N per task is small (17 to 20 train, 29 to 30 eval).
- App. C (pp. 9 to 10): the top four raw-bank artifacts per task in full. tablejoin's top four (a4, a9, a49, a1) are selected on 97% to 100% of runs and all say "match by values". Authors explicitly do not report per-artifact accuracy effects because selection is confounded with instance.

## Limitations

Theirs (Limitations section).
- One benchmark (LiveBench) only.
- Single-turn tasks only. They say agentic, long-horizon tasks are not covered and may be where an on-demand bank matters most.
- One non-Claude student and only Claude teachers.

Mine.
- Small eval sets: 29 to 30 instances per task, 119 pooled. Per-task gains of 0.06 to 0.07 (the cleaning losses) are within plausible noise; the authors admit this for spatial.
- Temperature 1 and 5 runs per instance. Run-level variance is averaged away, not reported.
- No thinking mode. Whether text artifacts help reasoning-mode models is untested.
- The selection stage is never ablated. We cannot tell whether gains come from the content or from the two-call structure, and we cannot tell what "give all artifacts" or "give no selection" would do.
- No placebo control. There is no generic or irrelevant artifact condition, so a prompt-length or "be careful" effect is not ruled out.
- No few-shot or retrieved-solved-example baseline, though RAG and few-shot are named as the alternatives.
- The headline cost comparison (60%, 89%) leaves out bank-building cost; only App. B's break-even folds it in, for one task.
- Teacher, judge and one student are the same model (Opus 4.5), so the "teacher benefits from its own bank" result mixes self-help with judge bias.
- The reflection pass shows the teacher the ground truth. Artifacts from failed training instances are written with the answer in view. Leakage is screened by the judge, not tested.
- Students differ in strength but the paper never plots gain against baseline accuracy per task.

## Untested assumptions

- That a student-agnostic bank is good enough. No student-tailored bank is compared.
- That artifacts which never helped any student should still be kept. There is no keep-only-if-it-helps gate.
- That text is the right form. No code, tool or worked-example form is tried.
- That the selector picks well. Selection accuracy is not measured against an oracle.
- That cleaning for concision is neutral. Their own data says it is not, but no alternative cleaning rule is tested.
- That LiveBench subtypes stand in for "repeating scenarios". Recurrence is argued from qualitative artifact analysis, not measured.

## Overlap with Loopy (K1 to K6)

What exactly is the artifact? Text only: an id, a one-line trigger and free-text advice. No code.
Does it compare artifact forms on the same students? No. The only contrast is raw text bank vs judge-cleaned text bank vs no bank.
Across how many students and of what strength? Four: Haiku 4.5 (weakest pooled .580), Sonnet 4.5 (.703), Qwen3.5-27B (.730), Opus 4.5 (.869, the teacher).
Does any form help weak models more? For the one form, yes in pooled terms: Haiku +0.071 and Qwen +0.070 vs Sonnet +0.020 and Opus +0.026. But Qwen is pooled-stronger than Sonnet and still gains like Haiku, because its gain sits on its one weak task (tablejoin .408). So the gain tracks per-task headroom plus a recurring missing step, not global model strength.
Any case where an artifact hurts? Yes, all text: Sonnet math_comp -0.048, Opus connections -0.019, Haiku connections -0.010 to -0.022, and the cleaned bank causing contradictory selections and lower precision for Haiku on tablejoin.

- K1 (text vs code, same failure, same teacher, same student): NOT covered. Text only.
- K2 (student-capability axis, form by strength interaction): PARTLY. Four students of different strength, one form. The gain of text shrinks for stronger Claude students (Table 1, §4). No second form, so no interaction or crossover can be shown.
- K3 (difficulty axis, text fades while code holds): NOT covered. Closest point: math_comp has the largest gap and zero transfer because artifacts are too narrow and the selector returns NONE 75% of the time (§4.1). That is about recurrence, not difficulty within a task.
- K4 (harm to capable students from executable or non-text help): NOT covered for non-text. Text harm on capable students is visible in Table 1 (Sonnet math_comp, Opus connections) but undiscussed.
- K5 (controls): PARTLY. Held-out instances of the same task types with near-duplicates kept on one side is a form of "variants of a repeating task". No generic placebo note. No keep-only-if-student-re-run-passes gate; the only filter is an LLM judge, and it hurt.
- K6 (cost and break-even with failed builds): PARTLY. Bank build cost per task and a break-even of about 170 queries on spatial (App. B). No failed builds exist in this design, and the cleaning call is not costed.

How it differs from Loopy's arms. Loopy's tailored checked note is written from the student's own failure and kept only if the student then passes. This paper's artifacts are written from the teacher's own solves and never checked on a student. It is closer to Loopy's generic note arm than to the tailored arm. The paper names TPD (Wang et al., 2024) and RICP (Sun et al., 2024) as the student-error-derived precedents.

## What we can no longer claim

- That teacher-written text guidance transferring to frozen smaller students without training is new.
- That a cheap student plus a text bank can match a bigger unaided model at lower cost on some tasks.
- That text guidance helps weaker students more than stronger ones in pooled terms (for text alone, single-turn).
- That gap size does not predict whether guidance transfers, and that guidance needs recurrence plus a missing step.
- That deduplicating or condensing notes can hurt a weak student.
- That a guidance bank has a computable break-even in number of uses.

## What stays open

- K1: text vs executable function from the same failure, same teacher, same student, head to head. Not touched.
- K2 as an interaction: whether the best form flips with student strength. This paper has one form, so it cannot answer.
- Agentic, multi-turn tasks such as AppWorld. The authors name this as their gap.
- A keep-only-if-the-student-passes gate and a generic placebo note. Neither is tested.
- Harm from executable help on students who already passed.
- Break-even that counts failed builds.

Lead to follow (cited by 2610.08775, not by this paper): Huang et al. 2026, arXiv 2608.11338, "Better, Faster, Stronger: Programmatic Skill Learning Best Reduces Agent Cost". Its title claims programs beat other skill forms on cost. It may be the closest K1 and K6 threat. Not read here.
