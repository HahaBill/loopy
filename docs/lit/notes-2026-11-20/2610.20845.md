# Evolving Together, Failing Apart: The Skill-Policy Co-evolution Trap in Self-Improving Agents

- arXiv: 2610.20845v1 [cs.AI], 14 Oct 2026. Feng, Wang, Yang, Shi, Xie, Yu, Wu, Ye (Alibaba Token Hub; Hong Kong Polytechnic University; University of Illinois at Chicago). Preprint, under review.
- Read on 2026-11-20 from the arXiv HTML text dump (3488 lines) and the 35-page PDF.
- Section, table and figure pointers below refer to the paper.

## Claim

Some methods train a policy model and a text skill bank together ("co-evolution"). They report success on the final bank only. The paper shows that the trained policy becomes tied to the exact text of that bank. If the skills are later rewritten, most of the gain from skills is lost.

Key numbers (Table 2, Section 4.4):
- Across 4 methods and 6 settings, worst-case success after a content rewrite is 4.9 to 12.6 points below the peak. That erases 52% to 93% of the bank's gain over the no-skill baseline.
- A rewrite that keeps the meaning (T1) flips the outcome of 13% to 23% of tasks. Decoding noise alone flips 6% to 8% (consistency rate CR 77.5 to 87.0 vs noise floor CR0 91.9 to 94.4).
- In 3 of 6 settings the meaning-preserving rewrite hurts more than an incomplete or a misleading rewrite.
- Sensitivity grows during training in 5 of 6 settings. 12 of 18 axis-setting slopes are significantly positive, four each on T1, T2, T3 (Section 3.3, Appendix H.1).

## Method (enough to reproduce)

Theory (Section 2, Appendix D):
- J(theta, S) = expected binary success of policy theta with bank S in context (Eq. 1).
- Assumption 1: each round keeps the candidate bank that is best for the current policy ("cooperative acceptance", Eq. 2). Assumption 2: the final candidate set covers all content rewrites of the delivered bank (Eq. 3).
- Proposition 1: under both, no content rewrite of the delivered bank can raise the delivered policy's success. A policy that was not trained against this bank is not bound by this; a rewrite may help it.
- Proposition 2: expected success over a distribution of rewritten banks = peak minus average degradation (Eq. 6). Training on one bank sets that second term to zero by construction.
- Construction 1 (D.3): a two-channel linear-Gaussian toy. One "semantic" channel with fixed strength, one "form" channel whose strength the bank step raises. Peak success and fragility both rise with the form signal (Proposition 3).
- D.5: if the gate keeps the bank that is worst for the policy (adversarial), the sign flips and fragility at the kept bank is zero.

Five-axis probe (Section 3.1, Appendix E.3 to E.7, examples in Appendix F):
- T1 semantic: paraphrase, same meaning. T2 sufficiency: delete a needed step or inject irrelevant text. T3 misleading: replace the strategy with a wrong one. T4 ecosystem: add, remove or swap skills. T5 autonomy: remove the bank.
- Rewriter: gpt-4o-2024-08-06, temperature 0.7, with a five-layer prompt (Table 3): domain context, protected action tokens, task category, skill tag, then the axis instruction. Requested length within plus or minus 30%; a field is regenerated once if the length ratio is outside [0.5, 2.0].
- Selection masking: for T1 to T3, run retrieval once on the original bank, record which skills were selected, then swap only their content fields. Retrieval-side fields (name, keywords, embedding, score) are frozen. T4 and T5 re-run retrieval.
- Checks are structural only. Nothing checks that T3 is really wrong or that T2 is really insufficient. T1 was audited after the fact (H.6): all 190 T1 rewrites judged meaning-equivalent by qwen3-max and gpt-4o, blinded, with 20 identity and 40 negative controls; a human re-checked 95.

Fragility curve (Section 3.2): hold the final bank and its rewritten versions fixed. Evaluate saved policy checkpoints along one training run, from the start to the peak. Drop_a(t) = SR(bank) minus SR(rewritten bank a) (Eq. 7). RP_a(t) = SR(rewritten) / SR(bank) (Eq. 8). Test the OLS slope of Drop over checkpoints.

CODA audit of the final pair (Section 4, Appendix E.8). All comparisons are paired on the same tasks, with per-episode logs:
- Gate: net skill benefit = SR(bank) minus SR(no bank) must be positive (Eq. 9).
- CR (consistency rate) = share of tasks whose outcome is the same under T1 as under the original bank (Eq. 10). Pass if CR >= CR0 minus 2 sd, where CR0 is the agreement between two unperturbed runs.
- RR_a (resistance rate) = share of tasks solved both with no skills and under corruption a, for T2 and T3 (Eq. 11). Pass if SR(corrupted) >= SR(no bank), McNemar test (Eq. 12).
- SR_min = lowest mean success over T1, T2, T3 (Eq. 13).

## Setting, benchmarks, models

- SkillRL on ALFWorld (140 tasks) and WebShop (500 tasks), backbone Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct.
- ReSkill on ScienceWorld (155 validation points) and ALFWorld, backbone Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507.
- Evolving-RL on Mind2Web, backbone Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct.
- ARISE on DeepScaleR-AMC23 (competition math, not interactive), backbone Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507.
- All students are RL fine-tuned. Skills are natural-language text injected into the prompt (15% to 40% of the prompt, H.5). No executable skills.
- Bank sizes (Table 14): SkillRL-ALFWorld 55 human-written seed skills growing to 91; SkillRL-WebShop 66 to 84; ReSkill 0 to 7 (ScienceWorld) and 0 to 8 (ALFWorld). Evolving-RL 200 skills, ARISE 110.
- Rollout temperature 0.4. Three evaluation repeats (five for ReSkill-ScienceWorld). One training run per setting, so error bars are evaluation noise only.

## Results, including weak and negative ones

Table 2, final pairs (peak / worst case / no-skill):
- SkillRL-ALFWorld 88.8 / 78.6 / 74.5. T1 78.6, T2 79.3, T3 79.5. CR 87.0 vs CR0 92.9.
- SkillRL-WebShop 52.4 / 47.4 / 45.9. T1 51.5, T2 47.4, T3 51.7. CR 83.2 vs 91.9.
- ReSkill-ScienceWorld 50.3 / 39.4 / 34.2. T1 39.4, T2 47.7, T3 47.7. CR 86.1 vs 93.6.
- ReSkill-ALFWorld 76.9 / 64.3 / 63.3. T1 64.3, T2 66.6, T3 65.4. CR 84.2 vs 92.2.
- Evolving-RL-Mind2Web 25.2 / 20.3 / 15.8. T1 23.2, T2 20.3, T3 20.4. CR 77.5 vs 94.4.
- ARISE-AMC23 87.4 / 80.9 / 75.3. T1 82.5, T2 81.5, T3 80.9. CR 82.1 vs 93.7.
- Resistance rates RR_T2 / RR_T3: 70.2/71.2, 37.8/40.0, 31.0/33.5, 60.6/58.9, 12.2/11.4, 63.5/65.4.

Verdict: every setting fails the consistency check. Every setting passes the gate and the harmful-axis check, so even a corrupted bank is no worse than no bank for the final policy.

Skills that hurt (Tables 8 to 13, the early-checkpoint rows). These matter most for Loopy:
- SkillRL-ALFWorld, base policy s0 (plain Qwen2.5-7B): 5.2% with the final 91-skill bank, 21.7% with no bank. With the misleading bank: 12.1%. So correct skills cut the base model to a quarter of its own score, and wrong skills did better than correct ones (RP_T3 = 2.33 at s0, 3.38 at s5; H.3).
- ReSkill-ScienceWorld s60: 28.5 with bank, 45.8 without, 45.4 with the misleading bank.
- ReSkill-ALFWorld s30: 20.0 with bank, 33.6 without.
- ARISE-AMC23 s100: 15.5 with bank, 17.2 without (small).
- Evolving-RL-Mind2Web is the exception: the bank helps from the first checkpoint (16.4 vs 14.0).
- The bank starts to help only after training: on SkillRL-ALFWorld the T5 drop turns positive around s40 (Figure 5).

Other points:
- At the final checkpoint, a misleading bank scores about the same as a paraphrased one (SkillRL-ALFWorld 79.5 vs 78.6) and still beats no bank. The trained policy reacts to whether the text is the text it trained on, far more than to whether the advice is right.
- Surface-form ratio kappa = T1 drop / T3 drop: 0.78 (SkillRL-ALFWorld), 2.14 (ReSkill-ScienceWorld); undefined on the other two SkillRL/ReSkill settings (H.5).
- H.4: across 32 checkpoints, "dependence" (success lost when the bank is removed) and "fragility" (mean drop over the five axes) correlate, Spearman rho = +0.82 [0.68, 0.89], slope +0.20 [0.15, 0.28] (Figure 16).
- H.7: the co-evolved SkillRL-ALFWorld policy without its bank scores 57.6%. A policy trained by RL with no skills at all averages 70.6% over its last five checkpoints (peak 87.5%, then collapses to 53.1%). So training with skills left the policy worse on its own. The authors call this a supporting observation; the two evaluations differ in task count (64 vs 140) and ALFWorld backend.
- Weak or negative: SkillRL-WebShop has no significant slope on any content axis (T3 p = 0.351). ARISE-AMC23 T3 slope p = 0.175. Training curves are not monotone (ReSkill-ALFWorld falls 61.0 to 26.9 between s180 and s240). ReSkill-ScienceWorld has only five checkpoints and wild cells (T1 = 2.7% at s240).

## Limitations

Authors' (Appendix A):
- L1. LLM rewriting is not bit-reproducible. If the bank was written by the same model family as the rewriter, fragility may be understated.
- L2. They diagnose and do not fix. Training over a distribution of banks (Eq. 6) is future work.
- L3. Scale is unstudied: more benchmarks, larger banks, more data.
- Also stated: one training run per setting; backbones differ by method, so numbers describe method plus backbone; no check tests meaning for T2 and T3; the toy model predicts misleading edits hurt more than paraphrases, which the data do not show and they did not test (Remark 4).

Mine:
- Only two small backbones (4B, 7B), each tied to a method. No model-size comparison at all.
- All students are RL fine-tuned on the bank. Nothing here is about a frozen off-the-shelf model given a note once. The headline fragility may not transfer to our setting. The early-checkpoint rows are the closest match and they are a side result.
- The base-policy harm (5.2% vs 21.7%) is reported without any look at trajectories. It could be context overload from long injected text, a prompt-format mismatch, or failure to carry out the advice. The paper does not say which.
- Figure 16 is partly circular. The y value is the mean drop over five axes including T5, and x is the T5 drop (Figure 4's legend labels the two averages "5 axes" and "w/o T5"). If the other four drops were unrelated to T5, the slope would be exactly 0.20, which is the pooled slope they report. The ALFWorld slope (0.18) adds nothing beyond that; only WebShop (0.37) does.
- Figure 16 uses 18 and 14 checkpoints while Tables 8 and 9 list 16 and 7. H.4 mentions an "s80 to s110 valley" on WebShop but Table 9 stops at s70. Appendix G says both T3 strengths are in the full tables; the tables show one T3 column. H.7 uses a "step-115 bank" and 84.1% where the report card says 88.8%. These look like results from different runs mixed together.
- T3 strength was chosen per setting (milder on ALFWorld, stronger on WebShop and ScienceWorld) with no stated rule.
- "18/18 endpoint pairs obey the prediction" is a weak test: it only needs rewrites not to beat the peak by more than noise at the single best checkpoint.

## Assumptions not tested

- Assumption 1 (the gate picks the exact best bank for the current policy) and Assumption 2 (the candidate set covers every content rewrite). The authors say they test the prediction, not the premises.
- That drift in real deployments looks like a GPT-4o paraphrase of every selected skill at once.
- That the loss under T1 comes from "reading form instead of meaning" as in the toy model. No mechanistic or trajectory evidence.
- That checkpoints along one run are comparable "doses" of co-evolution.

## Mapping onto Loopy components

- C1 (forms of transferred knowledge): not covered. Every skill is text in context. Programmatic skills appear only as a citation in related work (Shao et al. 2026, Appendix B).
- C2 (student strength): partly, in an indirect way. Strength varies along training checkpoints of one model, not across model sizes. The same bank hurts weak early checkpoints and helps strong late ones (Tables 8, 10, 11, 13; Figures 5, 8). L3 says scale is unstudied.
- C3 (predict the remedy before paying): partly at best. Measured dependence predicts fragility (H.4, Figure 16), with the circularity noted above. The net-benefit gate and kappa are after-the-fact diagnostics. Nothing predicts which remedy will work before running it.
- C6 (harm from skill use): covered, for text skills and RL-trained students. Correct skills that hurt a non-adapted policy (Table 8 s0: 5.2 vs 21.7; Table 10 s60; Table 11 s30). Meaning-preserving rewrites that flip 13 to 23% of outcomes (Table 2). Skill training that erodes stand-alone ability (H.7, Figure 17). Metrics for harm: CR, RR, SR_min, Drop, RP.
- C7 (execution gap): partly. The paper never frames it as "has the procedure, cannot execute". But its base-policy rows show a 7B model that does worse with a bank of correct procedures than with none, and no worse with misleading ones. That is evidence the model is not acting on the content. The offered explanation is fit between policy and bank (Proposition 1 third clause, Corollary 1: a policy "reading a channel incorrectly"). No trajectory analysis, no size dependence.

## What this means for Loopy

What we can no longer claim:
- That "a correct text skill can make a weak agent worse" is new. Here a bank of correct skills cut a base Qwen2.5-7B on ALFWorld from 21.7% to 5.2% (Table 8).
- That the value of a note is a property of the note. The same text helps one checkpoint and hurts another. Any COACH result is a result about a note-reader pair.
- That a COACH failure shows the small model "knows the steps and cannot do them" without a wording control. Here a paraphrase alone moved 13 to 23% of outcomes, though for RL-trained readers.

What is still open after this paper:
- No comparison of text advice with an executable function. No frozen-model, one-shot note setting. No model-size axis. No explanation of why correct skills hurt the base model.

Design lessons to copy:
- Corrupted-note controls. For each coach failure, also run an incomplete note (T2) and a misleading note (T3) on the same task. If the small model scores the same under all three, it is not using the note's content. If the correct note beats the corrupted ones and still fails, the model uses the note and cannot finish. This separates "ignores the advice" from "cannot execute the advice", which is our C7 question.
- Paraphrase control. Run each coach note in one or two meaning-preserving rewrites before calling a failure a capability failure.
- Noise floor. Run every arm twice and report the flip rate between identical runs (6 to 8% here at temperature 0.4). Our 9-task pilot cannot separate 1/6 from noise.
- Paired design with per-episode logs: same tasks in every arm, a no-note arm always, McNemar on paired outcomes, and a net-benefit gate (note minus no note) before any other claim.
- Hold selection fixed when changing content, so a change in what gets retrieved is not mistaken for a change in what the text does.
- Report consistency (share of tasks with unchanged outcome), not only the change in success rate. Flips in both directions cancel in the average.

Experiment that would extend it:
- Run the T1 to T3 audit on COACH and COMPILE for a frozen small model on AppWorld. For a function the analogs are: an equivalent refactor or rename (T1), a version missing one step (T2), a version with wrong logic (T3). The prediction to test: COMPILE is flat under T1 and collapses under T2 and T3, while COACH for a weak model may be flat under all three because the content is not being used.
- Add the model-size axis the authors leave open (L3): the same notes and functions across three or four student sizes.
- Check whether a frozen, non-adapted model shows the two-sided response the theory allows (rewrites that help), and whether that could be used to pick a wording the student can act on.
