# 2611.06955 — A Sharp Additive Bound for the Threshold Rule in Repeated Rent-or-Buy

Vasco Schiavo. arXiv:2611.06955v1 [cs.DS], 3 Nov 2026. 16 pages. Single author. Pure theory, no experiments.
Source read: `lit_fulltext/2611.06955.txt` (full) and the PDF pages 1-16.

## Claim

Theorem 1 (Section 1, proved in Sections 3 and 4). Fix a reset price K > 0. Let a session have P runs (a run is a maximal block of equal consecutive increments).

1. The threshold rule pays at most `min{ 2·OPT, OPT + (2P-1)·K }`. OPT is the best schedule chosen with hindsight.
2. The coefficient 2P-1 is sharp. For every eta > 0 there is a session with exactly P runs where the rule loses more than (2P-1-eta)·K.

In words: one reset price for not knowing the session, plus two reset prices for each change of growth rate. The rule knows neither the runs, nor P, nor the horizon T.

Other results:

- Remark 9. With constant growth (P = 1) every deterministic rule loses at least K on some session. So the base term cannot be improved.
- Remark 11. If every increment is within a factor (1+eps) of a session with P runs, cost is at most `(1+eps)·(OPT + (2P-1)K) + eps·K`.
- Theorem 15. A "drop-aware" rule (threshold rule that also resets when the increment decreases) pays at most `OPT + (1 + P_minus + 2·P_plus)·K`. P_minus and P_plus count decreases and increases.
- Theorem 16. No deterministic rule guarantees a loss below 2K on sessions with at most two runs (one increase).
- Theorem 18. On two runs the threshold rule loses at most 2K if the change is an increase, but can lose close to 3K on a decrease. The drop-aware rule loses at most 2K in every two-run case. So 2K is optimal there and the drop-aware rule achieves it.

## Setting and assumptions (exact)

- One session with calls t = 1..T. T is unknown to the rule.
- State is a history h_t. h_1 = 0. Each call t < T appends an increment a_t >= 0.
- Every call pays h_t as rent. That is the history accumulated since the last reset.
- Before any call the rule may reset. A reset costs a fixed K and clears the history. The reset call still pays h_t (the reset reads what it clears). Remark 10 shows the bound also holds if a reset call pays K only.
- Cost of a schedule: sum over calls of `r_t·K + h_t`.
- Rules are deterministic maps from the observed increments a_1..a_{t-1} to {reset, no reset}.
- The threshold rule (Definition 5): keep an accumulator `acc` of rent paid on non-reset calls since the last reset. Reset when `acc + h_t >= K`.
- No terminal reset is required. This differs from standard TCP acknowledgment.

Stated assumptions (Section 1, first paragraph):

- Increments are the same under every schedule. A reset does not change what the agent does later.
- K is fixed.
- A reset fully clears the modeled history.
- Left out on purpose: the effect of a summary on the information the agent keeps, prompt caching, and summaries whose size depends on their content. "The theorems bound the modeled cost only."

The buy here is a recurring reset, not a one-time purchase. Rent grows between resets. This is the dynamic TCP acknowledgment / lot sizing / economic order quantity family. It is not classic ski rental and not Bahncard.

## Proof idea (enough to reuse)

Single-rate quantities, for a constant rate alpha:

- `g_alpha(n) = alpha·n(n+1)/2`. Rent of the first n calls after a reset.
- `f_alpha(n) = (K + g_alpha(n))/n`. Cost per call if you reset every n calls.
- `L_alpha = min{n : g_alpha(n) >= K}`. `f*_alpha = f_alpha(L_alpha)`. Proposition 6(2): this is the minimum of f_alpha. So under constant growth the threshold rule uses the optimal period (a discrete EOQ).
- Proposition 6(1): the rule is 2-competitive. Proof by splitting calls into the rule's cycles.

Potentials:

- Rule state is (e, acc). e is the carry (history kept after the previous decision). Invariant: `0 <= e <= acc < K`.
- `R_j(e) = g_alpha(j) + j·e`. Rent up to a reset at the j-th call from carry e.
- `V_j(e) = K + R_j(e) - j·f*_alpha`. Cost of the next cycle relative to the optimal average.
- `W_alpha(e) = min over j >= 0 of V_j(e)`, with the artificial `V_0 = K` so W is capped at K.
- `D_alpha(e, acc) = V_k(e)` where k is the rule's crossing index (first k with `acc + R_k(e) >= K`).

Lemma 7: W is in [0, K], W(0) = 0, W is nondecreasing in e, and `0 <= D - W(e) <= K` whenever `e <= acc < K`. One-step identity: `V_{j+1}(e) = V_j(e+alpha) + e + alpha - f*`.

Lemma 8, per call at rate alpha:

- Rule: `cost_t - f* = D(before) - D(after)`. Exact.
- Any comparator with carries o, o': `cost_t - f* >= W(o) - W(o')`.

Main argument. Let `Z = D_alpha(e, acc) - W_alpha(o)`. The excess cost of a call is at most `Z_t - Z_{t+1}`. Sum over calls. Inside a run the Z terms telescope. What is left:

- Start term: 0.
- End term: at most K (since Z >= -K).
- Each rate change: the old-rate Z and new-rate Z are evaluated at the same carries e and o. Both lie in the same interval of width 2K ([-K, K] if e <= o, [0, 2K] if e > o). So a change adds at most 2K.

Total: `K + 2K·(P-1) = (2P-1)K`.

Sharpness (Section 4). Take K = 1. Build P runs with fast-shrinking rates: `alpha_i = 2/(L_i(L_i+1))`, `L_i = (j_i+1)^2`, `j_i = 1/alpha_{i-1} - 1`. The rule enters each slower run with stale carry alpha_{i-1}. It pays almost K in repeated rent on that carry, then pays a reset, then one more. The comparator resets right before each boundary and enters every run with carry 0. Loss exceeds `2P - 1 - 2/(L_1+1) - 6(P-1)/(L_1(L_1+1))`. All sharp instances change by decreases.

Drop-aware bound (Theorem 15) adds Lemma 14: `W_alpha(e)` is nonincreasing in alpha. A forced reset at a decrease then costs at most K.

Lower bound (Theorem 16). Adversary feeds a slow constant rate `alpha = 3K/(n(n-1))`. If the rule never resets in 2n calls, stop: it lost 2K. If it resets, append a burst of 2K right after. Either a second reset or carrying the burst costs about 2K. The rule cannot tell "slow growth continues" from "slow growth then a burst".

Two-run upper bound for increases (Lemma 17, Theorem 18). A threshold cycle of m calls at rate alpha then k calls at a higher rate beta costs at most its fair price `m·f*_alpha + k·f*_beta`. Long algebra with a case split on a sign.

## Results, including weak and negative ones

- The additive bound beats the ratio 2 only when `(2P-1)K < OPT`. Then the ratio is `1 + (2P-1)K/OPT`.
- The factor-2 worst case comes from sessions whose optimum is about K (one small burst, then stop right after the reset). On those the rule loses exactly K.
- The threshold rule's extra loss comes from decreases, not increases (two-run case).
- Negative: the drop-aware rule is not c-competitive for any constant c. If growth alternates between alpha and alpha-eps it resets every other call. Its ratio grows like `sqrt(K/(8·alpha))`.
- Negative: the sharpness instances are doubly exponential in P (Remark 12: `L_{i+1} = (L_i(L_i+1)/2)^2`). With a bounded horizon or a minimum positive rate the best coefficient is unknown.
- Open (end of Section 5): a rule with loss at most P·K for P >= 3? The sharp bound under repeated increases? A rule with 2K on two runs that is also constant-competitive? Is the eps·K term in Remark 11 needed?

## Predictions, failure, multiple items, citations (direct answers)

- Can the purchase fail or have random value? No. A reset always clears the history and costs exactly K. Nothing is stochastic.
- More than one thing to buy? No. One action, one price. The drop-aware rule is a second rule, not a second purchasable item.
- Any prediction or advice? No. The intro says the guarantee holds "with no predictions or stochastic assumptions". Learning-augmented work is cited only as related work: Purohit et al. 2018 [20], Bamas et al. 2020 [6], Im et al. 2023 [14], Angelopoulos et al. 2020 [2].
- Does it cite PACE (2610.02932)? No. PFSUM (2410.15257)? No. Checked by grep and by reading the reference list.
- Does it cite LLM agents? Yes. The motivation is context compaction: when should an agent summarize its history? Cites CADOC [24, arXiv 2609.37012], CliffCompaction [19, 2609.26779], Self-GC [10, 2607.00692], AutoCompact [25, 2610.02163], token-cost studies [5, 2604.22750], [23, 2607.12161], and prompt caching [17, 2601.06007]. It says the threshold rule itself is the commit rule of CADOC. The new part is the guarantee.
- Would the bound apply to "two purchasable artifacts with different prices and success rates"? No. The proof needs a single fixed K, a reset that always returns the state to (0, 0), and increments that do not depend on the schedule. The invariant `e <= acc < K` and the potential W are built from one K. A second item with another price, or a reset that works only with some probability, breaks Lemma 7(4) and the 2K jump bound. The paper offers no extension and does not list one among its open questions.

## Limitations

Authors':

- Bounds the modeled cost only. Summary quality, caching and variable summary size are out.
- Sharpness needs very long sessions.
- Drop-aware rule gives up constant competitiveness.

Mine:

- No experiment. Nobody checks that real agent histories grow in a few constant-rate runs. P counts exact changes, so on real traces P is close to T and the bound is empty. Remark 11 helps only if a small-P session fits within a factor (1+eps), and the paper does not study that trade-off.
- Real per-call cost with prompt caching is not h_t.
- Deterministic rules only. No randomized rule, although randomization gives e/(e-1) for standard TCP acknowledgment.
- Single author, v1, LLM-assisted (disclosed). I followed the main proof and the sharpness arithmetic and found no error. I did not re-derive the Lemma 17 expansion line by line.

## Assumptions not tested

- A reset does not change later increments (a summarized agent behaves the same).
- A reset does not hurt task success.
- K is constant across the session.
- Growth is piecewise constant with small P.

## Mapping to Loopy components

- C3 (self-predictions and their accuracy): not covered. No predictions at all.
- C4 (economics over repeats): partly. Gives a break-even threshold rule and a sharp additive guarantee with its assumptions (Theorem 1, Remark 9, Theorem 16). But the buy is a recurring full reset at a fixed price. No failed buys. No variable build cost. No per-use cost after buying.

## What this means for Loopy

- It is not our setting. Loopy's compile is a one-time purchase with a small per-use cost after it ($0.001) and a chance to fail. That is closer to Bahncard with failure. This paper is the recurring-reset branch.
- It still confirms the gap. The newest LLM-motivated rent-or-buy theory has one purchasable item, a deterministic purchase, a fixed price and no advice. "Two artifacts with different prices and success rates" is open here too.
- Useful framing 1: report additive loss in units of the build cost K, not a ratio. The paper shows the ratio 2 is driven by sessions where OPT is about K. Our break-even is 1 to 2 repeats, so OPT is about K for most task families. A ratio guarantee says little there. "We lose at most c build costs" says more. (My inference, not a result of the paper.)
- Useful framing 2: charge the loss to the number of regime changes, not to the horizon. If a compiled function goes stale when the task drifts, a recompile is a reset and P is the number of drifts. The 2K-per-change price is the template.
- Useful baseline: the accumulator rule "buy when rent paid since the last buy reaches K". With our pilot numbers (rent $0.015 per task, build $0.007 to $0.033) it fires after 1 to 3 rented tasks. That is my arithmetic under a constant-rent reading. The paper's theorem does not cover that cost structure.
- Theorem 16 is the no-advice impossibility in miniature: no deterministic rule can tell "this stays cheap" from "this is about to get expensive". Our small model's forecast is uninformative, so we are in that no-advice regime and should expect to pay about one K for not knowing.
- The (1+eps) in Remark 11 is a perturbation of the input. It is unrelated to PACE's (1+eps) budget guarantee. Do not conflate them.
- Cite as: nearest LLM-motivated additive guarantee, deterministic single purchase. Follow up CADOC [24] only if we model stale artifacts.
