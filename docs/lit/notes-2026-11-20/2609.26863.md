# 2609.26863 · Towards Strategy-Level RSI for Skill-Augmented Agents: Learning When to Reuse Skills from Execution Feedback

Guo, Sui, Jiang, Li, Li, Li, Ou (Central China Normal University, Beijing Kuafu Technology, Monash). arXiv v3, 9 Oct 2026. 21 pages. Code: github.com/kuafuai/SkillApt.
Source read: arXiv HTML full text (1383 lines) plus all 21 PDF pages for figures and tables.

## Claim (one paragraph)

A skill that is relevant to a task is not always worth loading. Given a candidate skill, decide Load or Abstain from stored paired outcomes: the same task state run WITH and WITHOUT the skill. The model, the agent and the skill text stay fixed. Only the loading policy changes. Key number (Table 1, Sec 5.1): on 20 skills and 160 held-out states, SkillApt reaches 91.3% task success, up from 81.9% for "never load", equal to a zero-shot LLM controller (91.3%). But SkillApt loads the skill on 26.3% of states. The zero-shot controller loads on 98.8%. So the gain over a strong baseline is not accuracy. It is fewer loads and fewer tokens (1,188.5 vs 1,811.7 selected-arm tokens).

## Method, in enough detail to reproduce

**Setting** (Sec 3.1, 3.2). Policy π(a | z, S_i, m, E_i), a in {Load, Abstain}. z is the task state. In the experiments z is just the task question text. S_i is the candidate skill. m is the executor model. E_i is the evidence store for that skill and that model.

**Paired evidence** (Sec 3.3). For each evidence state run both arms with the same prompt shell and evaluator. The WITH arm adds the skill text to the context. It does not force the model to follow it. One pair per state, no repeats. Label, correctness first:
- correctness-positive: WITH right, WITHOUT wrong.
- correctness-harmful: WITH wrong, WITHOUT right.
- tie: both right or both wrong. Ties are then split by cost. Relative cost advantage g = mean over {tokens, latency, calls} of (cost WITHOUT - cost WITH) / max(1, cost WITHOUT). Cost-positive if g > 0.05, cost-negative if g < -0.05, else neutral (Eq 6).

**Estimator** (Sec 3.6). Represent the question by lowercased whitespace unigrams and bigrams, signed SHA-256 hashing into 256 dimensions, unit norm. For a new state take the five nearest evidence states of the same skill and same model by cosine (floored at zero). Target y = 1 for correctness-positive, 0 for harmful, 0.5 for any tie (Eq 9). Score p = (0.5 + Σ w·y) / (1 + Σ w) (Eq 10). Load if p > 0.5, else Abstain. With no evidence it abstains.

**Splits** (Sec 4.2, App A). SRA-Bench skill/task mappings for LogicBench and MedCalc-Bench. 10 skills per domain. 20 fresh states per skill: 12 evidence, 8 held out. 240 evidence states, 160 held-out. Split frozen before any run, seed 20260923. Same skills in evidence and test. Only the task states are new.

**Executors.** "gpt-5.6-terra" (provider-reported), temperature 0, low reasoning effort, 4,096 output tokens. Replication on Claude Sonnet 4.6 through a third-party OpenAI-compatible proxy, 10 skills, 80 held-out states.

**Comparators** (Sec 4.3). Cold Start (always abstain). Zero-shot LLM controller: the same strong model reads the task and the skill text and answers LOAD or ABSTAIN, no history (prompt in App A). WITH-only: same estimator, but the target is just "was the WITH arm correct". BASM-inspired D2: the model writes text applicability boundaries from the same evidence (256-token cap), then a model checker decides. Global-5: paired targets, first five evidence items, no similarity. Skill-Global-All: mean of all evidence of the skill. Logistic regression on [state hash, skill hash, cosine], 513 features.

**Phase B** (hard candidates). 200 tasks from CHAMP, LogicBench, MedCalc-Bench, TheoremQA. Three arms each: No Skill, Gold Skill, and the top non-gold skill by BM25. 199 complete triplets.

**Sequential replay** (Sec 5.4, App B.5). Over the 240 evidence states, 12 rounds per skill. At round r predict using only earlier states, record the chosen arm's outcome, then add the pair. 500 random within-skill orders as a robustness check.

## What signal decides reuse (answer to the specific question)

The signal is past paired correctness on similar states of the same skill under the same executor. Load only when the nearby evidence shows the skill turned wrong answers into right ones more than the reverse. It is not relevance, not the model's own judgment, not success-with-skill.

Three things the paper shows about this signal:
- Success-with-skill alone is a bad signal. Of 240 evidence states, 204 are right in both arms. Only 23 are right because of the skill, 7 are wrong because of it, 6 wrong in both. The success-only statistic is 0.946. The paired statistic is 0.533 (Eq 4, 5, Sec 3.3). A WITH-only policy loads on 99.4% of states.
- A strong LLM's zero-shot judgment is a bad signal too. It loads on 98.8% of states. Its agreement with the utility label is 27/152 (17.8%). It loads all 3 harmful held-out states (Table 4), and 19 of 20 harmful states in the fresh cohort (Table 12).
- State similarity adds little. Global-5 (no similarity) lands close to SkillApt, difference not significant (App B.3). The paired target is what matters.

## Is cost counted (answer to the specific question)

Partly, and not in the decision.
- Cost is measured per arm: tokens, latency, tool calls. It labels ties as cost-positive or cost-negative. Those labels feed the "policy accuracy" metric.
- The estimator ignores cost. All ties map to 0.5 (Eq 9). The authors say so (Sec 3.6, 6.2).
- Reported cost is "selected tokens": mean tokens of the chosen arm. It excludes the cost of building evidence (both arms, 12 states per skill) and the controller's own cost.
- Some overheads are given. The zero-shot controller costs 1,900.9 tokens per state on top (App B.1). The D2 boundary checker used 387,373 tokens for 160 calls (App B.3). Claude's 400 arms used 505,430 tokens (App A).
- No dollars. No break-even. The authors state: "selected-arm token savings do not establish amortized total savings without a specified evidence-reuse horizon" (Sec 7), and "When such evidence is worth acquiring remains open" (Sec 6.2).
- Token-only relabeling (App B.9): every cost-positive tie disappears. The cost-positive category came from latency noise.
- My rough arithmetic, not theirs: evidence for 20 skills is 240 states x two arms, about 640k tokens (using the evidence-stream means 866.5 and 1,806.5). SkillApt saves about 620 to 670 tokens per state against always-load. That is roughly 1,000 later states to pay back, about 50 per skill. If the WITH arm would have run anyway and only the WITHOUT arm is extra, it is about 310 states, about 16 per skill.

## Setting, benchmarks, models

LogicBench (logical reasoning) and MedCalc-Bench (medical calculation) for the main study. CHAMP and TheoremQA added for Phase B. All are direct question answering with a final-answer checker. Tool calls are zero. Skills are text injected into context. Executors are strong models: base success without any skill is 81.9% (Terra) and 65.0% (Claude).

## Metric

- Task success: held-out correctness of the arm the policy chose, read from frozen outcomes ("offline selected-arm task success").
- Activation: share of states where the policy chose Load.
- Selected tokens: mean tokens of the chosen arm.
- Policy accuracy: agreement with a utility label. Load is right for correctness-positive or tied cost-positive. Abstain is right for correctness-harmful or tied cost-negative. Neutral excluded. Denominator 152.
- Useful recall (loads among 25 utility-positive states), cost-negative avoidance (124 states), harm avoidance (3 states).
- Main interval: bootstrap over the 20 skill clusters, 10,000 resamples. McNemar exact for paired task outcomes.

## Results, including weak and negative ones

- SkillApt vs cold start: +9.4 points, 95% CI [2.5, 18.1]. 15 states improve, none worsen. The whole gain sits in 5 of 20 skills (Table 6).
- SkillApt vs zero-shot controller: success difference zero, CI [-3.1, +3.1]. All 116 disagreements are zero-shot Load vs SkillApt Abstain. 97 of them are states where the skill only adds cost.
- SkillApt never beats always-load on success. On the evidence stream Always Load gets 94.6%, sequential SkillApt 92.1% (App B.5). The gain over Always Load is ≤ 0 in every round. The benefit is 36.9% fewer selected tokens.
- Versus cold start SkillApt costs more: tokens 856 to 1,189, and policy accuracy falls from 83.6% to 77.0% because it loads on some cost-only states.
- Evidence budget (Table 5, Fig 2). 0 and 2 states per skill: nothing loads, 81.9%. 4 states: 86.9%, activation 26.9%. 6 states: 90.6%. 12 states: 91.3%, activation 26.3%. Not monotone in activation or policy accuracy.
- Hard candidates (Table 7, Fig 3). No Skill 81.4%, Gold Skill 83.4%, hard candidate 78.4%. Hard vs none: -3.0, CI [-7.0, +1.0], not significant. Hard candidate: 169 of 199 leave correctness unchanged and add cost, 12 turn right into wrong, 6 help. Only 6 of the 12 harms persist on rerun. Even the gold skill harms 9 tasks and only adds cost on 142.
- Ablations (Table 2, 4). WITH-only: 91.3% success, 99.4% activation, 17.1% policy accuracy. D2 text boundaries: 85.6%, 33.1%, 63.8%, significantly worse than SkillApt (+5.6 success, p = 0.012). Logistic regression: 83.8%, 20.0%, 75.7%. Global-5: 89.4%, 25.0%, 75.7%.
- Sequential replay (Fig 4, Table 9). Gain over round-matched cold start: +2.5 points in rounds 1 to 4, +6.25 in rounds 9 to 12. Each round is 20 states, so each step is 5 points. Late beats early in 78% of 500 orders. Interval for late minus early: [-3.8, +10.0].
- Threshold sweep (Fig 5). τ = 0.4 loads everywhere with no success gain. τ = 0.6 loads 10% and drops to 86.3%.
- Cross-model (Table 3, 10, 11). Terra +8.8 [3.75, 15.0]. Claude +5.0 [-3.75, 13.75], not significant. Claude's policy loaded all 5 of its harmful states. 17 of 80 states change their Load/Abstain label between the two models.
- Fresh cohort (Table 12). 12 new skills, 240 states, uneven evidence (1 to 22 pairs, median 5). SkillApt 87.1%, zero-shot 84.2%, cold start 82.1%, always load 84.6%. SkillApt vs zero-shot +2.9, p = 0.27, not significant. On MedCalc (1 to 4 pairs per skill) SkillApt never loaded, and loading hurt (63/80 vs 73/80).
- Retrieval study (App B.8). SkillApt equals BM25 Top-1 at 83.8% with activation 31.5% vs 100%.
- Reported failures. A stronger-retriever control failed its prerequisite (reranker R@10 0.21 vs BM25 0.51) and was stopped. A quality-judge extension was stopped because the judge was only 75% swap-consistent. A temporal replay on real software-agent data was stopped before any run. The first boundary baseline (D1) had a bug and was replaced by D2.
- Real software-agent fixtures (App C, Table 13). Dataset-contract repair: 100% to 100%, median tokens -18%. Global-import cleanup: 80% to 100%, -31%. 54 runs, spread up to 133% of a median. Supplementary only.

## Limitations

**Authors'** (Sec 7).
- Skills are text only. "Executable scripts, code Skills, hard runtime constraints, and tool-plugin Skills are not tested." (Sec 3.2)
- Final-answer evaluators. A tie means the checker saw no difference, not that outputs are equal.
- Two benchmarks. Generalizes to new states of known skills, not to new skills.
- One pair per state, so run-to-run noise stays.
- Evidence costs extra runs. Amortized savings not shown.
- The estimator does not learn the cost boundary, which is where most wasted loads are.
- Cross-model result is inconclusive. Model names are provider-reported.
- Offline replay only. No online learning, no drift detection.

**Mine.**
- The headline gain is against "never load", a weak baseline. Against always-load or the zero-shot controller there is no success gain anywhere, only fewer tokens.
- Small counts drive everything: 15 improved states in 5 skills, 3 harmful held-out states, 25 utility-positive states.
- The base rate is high (82% without any skill). This is the opposite regime to a weak student that fails alone.
- The state is the question text, with hashed word pairs. No environment state.
- In deployment the other arm is never seen. The evidence store cannot grow for free. The "sequential" replay still reads the unseen arm after each decision.
- The token saving ignores the evidence bill. By my rough count the payback is tens of reuses per skill.
- The Claude runs went through a third-party proxy.

## Assumptions not tested

- Skill utility is stable over time for a given skill and model.
- A single run per arm is a fair label (half of the screened harms did not repeat).
- Evidence from one executor does not transfer to another (asserted, and supported only by label disagreement on 17 of 80 states).
- Word-overlap similarity is a useful notion of "similar state" (their own Global-5 result suggests it adds little).
- Results carry to executable skills, tool tasks, or weak executors.

## Mapping to C1 to C6

- **C1 forms of transferred knowledge: not.** One form only, text skills, contents fixed. No program, no weights, no teacher.
- **C2 student strength: partly, by accident.** Two executors of different strength (base 82.5% vs 65.0% on the same 80 states). Utility labels differ on 17 of 80 states and the weaker one suffers more harm (Table 10, 11, App B.6). Not designed as a strength study.
- **C3 predict before paying: partly.** It predicts, before running, whether loading will help, from past paired outcomes (Sec 3.6). It also shows that zero-shot LLM judgment, success-only history, and model-written text boundaries predict poorly (Table 2, 4). But the prediction is Load vs Abstain for one remedy, and it needs paid evidence first (nothing loads with ≤ 2 pairs, Table 5).
- **C4 economics: partly.** Selected-arm tokens, some overheads, a token-only check (Table 1, App A, B.9). Evidence cost excluded. No break-even. No dollars. The gap is named by the authors.
- **C5 online per-task choice: partly.** A per-state Load/Abstain choice, tested by test-then-update replay (Sec 5.4, App B.5). Two options only. No rent, no compile.
- **C6 harm: covered.** Hard candidate harms 12/199 (6 persist), gold skill harms 9/199, 20/240 harmful in the fresh cohort, always-load on MedCalc 63/80 vs 73/80 without (Sec 5.2, Table 7, Table 12).

## What this means for Loopy

**We can no longer claim:**
- That a relevant skill can hurt or just add cost. Published with counts.
- That an LLM's prior judgment of whether help will work is uninformative. Shown here for a strong controller deciding on skill loading. Our pilot finding (the small model's self-forecast is uninformative, the cloud model labels everything "procedural") is in line with this, not new in kind. What is still ours: those two specific signals, and the three-way remedy choice.
- That "it succeeded with the skill" proves the skill helped. The WITH-only ablation covers it.
- That paired with/without runs are the right evidence for a reuse decision.

**Still open after this paper:**
- Executable skills. The authors exclude them in so many words. Our compile arm is outside their scope.
- A weak student. Their executors pass 65 to 82% unaided. Ours passes 0/9.
- Choosing among rent, coach and compile. They choose between a text skill and nothing.
- Payback over repeats, including the cost of building evidence and of failed builds. They name this as open.
- A decision rule that uses cost, not only correctness.

**Design lessons to copy:**
1. Always keep the no-help arm on the same task instance. Label each coach or compile result by the pair, not by success alone. Our compile 5/6 only means something next to small-alone on the same instances, rerun.
2. Rerun harms and wins. Half of their screened harms did not repeat at temperature zero.
3. Report three numbers apart: task success, how often the remedy was used, and agreement with the utility label. Success alone hid a 99% vs 26% difference in use.
4. Start the router at "do nothing" and say how much evidence moves it. Here nothing moved with 2 pairs per skill, and the curve flattened at about 6.
5. For any online claim use test-then-update replay, a round-matched baseline, and random orderings.
6. Count cost in tokens first. Their latency-based cost labels did not survive a token-only check.
7. Be wary of turning evidence into a text rule. Their model-written boundaries did worse than the raw outcome counts. That is a cousin of our coach note built from a diagnosis.
8. Report stopped and failed side studies as they did.
